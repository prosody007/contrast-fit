The useful middle

The best interface is not the loudest one. It is the one that helps the next decision arrive with less friction.

Good tools leave room for attention. They use color to mark a change in state, not to decorate every available surface. They give the reader a place to begin, a place to pause, and a clear way back.

Field note

When a background changes, every layer around it has a new job. Measure the relationship, then decide what deserves emphasis. Try a small practice.

Signal over noise

Hierarchy is a sequence: an anchor, a supporting detail, and one action that earns the eye. The palette follows that sequence with text, muted copy, and an intentional status pair.

A small practice

Start with the context your users actually enter. Keep the fixture concrete. Then export the tokens and let the host own its layout, semantics, and interaction model.
